Since digital breast tomosynthesis (DBT) was approved for clinical use by the FDA in 2011 and Medicare coverage began in 2015 , radiology practices and screening programs in the United States have rapidly adopted the technology. The most recent MQSA statistics suggest DBT-capable machines account for roughly 95% of installations in certified facilities nationally. Compared with full field digital mammography (FFDM), DBT maximizes the identification of biologically significant disease while mitigating overdiagnosis .
